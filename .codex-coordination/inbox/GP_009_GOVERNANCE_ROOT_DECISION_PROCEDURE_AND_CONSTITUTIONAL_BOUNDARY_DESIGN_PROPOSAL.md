DESIGN SCOPE 1: GOVERNANCE ROOT DECISION PROCEDURE

Study a possible procedure:

```text
PROPOSAL
    |
EVALUATION
    |
INDEPENDENT REVIEW
    |
DECISION
    |
RATIFICATION
    |
ACTIVATION
```

Each stage must remain distinct and independently attributable.

PROPOSAL STAGE QUESTIONS:

- who may propose a Trust Anchor or Governance Root candidate;
- which identity, authority, scope, and evidence must accompany the proposal;
- how the proposal binds a Contract version and governance context;
- how competing proposals are represented without implicit preference.

EVALUATION STAGE QUESTIONS:

- which mandatory criteria apply;
- which evidence is required;
- who performs evaluation;
- how conflicts of interest are disclosed;
- how incomplete or contradictory evidence causes a blocked result.

INDEPENDENT REVIEW STAGE QUESTIONS:

- which identity and runtime independence evidence is required;
- how Review authority is target- and scope-bound;
- how Formal Review remains distinct from Advisory input and Decision;
- how Reviewer conflict or unverifiable independence causes fail-closed behavior.

DECISION STAGE QUESTIONS:

- who may exercise Decision Authority;
- which evaluated and reviewed inputs are mandatory;
- how dissent, uncertainty, and material defects are represented;
- how Decision scope remains separate from ratification and activation.

RATIFICATION STAGE QUESTIONS:

- whether human confirmation, Contract confirmation, or both are required;
- whether quorum, multi-party confirmation, or time delay is appropriate;
- which constitutional boundaries must be validated before ratification;
- what evidence makes ratification durable and auditable.

ACTIVATION STAGE QUESTIONS:

- which separately authorized action activates the accepted root;
- how activation binds the exact Decision, ratification evidence, Contract
  version, scope, and effective time;
- how pre-activation actions remain prohibited;
- how activation failure or inconsistency causes no authority to become active.

PROCEDURE STATUS:
DEFINED FOR STUDY / NOT IMPLEMENTED

DESIGN SCOPE 2: CONSTITUTIONAL BOUNDARY

Study whether ACOS requires governance boundaries that cannot be changed through
ordinary operational authority.

CANDIDATE CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLES:

- Authority Separation;
- Identity Separation;
- Author, Executor, Materializer, Reviewer, Auditor, and Decision Authority
  attribution;
- least authority and no privilege escalation;
- target, hash, purpose, and scope binding;
- Review, Decision, Ratification, Activation, and Implementation separation;
- fail-closed behavior;
- durable audit requirements;
- historical integrity and no retrospective authority reconstruction;
- governed amendment and supersession;
- external Advisory non-binding status;
- operational authority never implied by governance source alone.

CONSTITUTIONAL BOUNDARY QUESTIONS:

- which principles are immutable, entrenched, or ordinarily amendable;
- which authority may propose an amendment;
- which Review and ratification are required;
- whether emergency exceptions are permitted;
- how an exception remains narrow, temporary, and audited;
- how conflicts between a future Constitution and Contract are resolved;
- how constitutional versioning and migration preserve historical evidence.

CONSTITUTIONAL BOUNDARY STATUS:
DESIGN SUBJECT / NOT ESTABLISHED
